At the edge of the valley, a narrow road followed the river toward a small town. Mara watched the hills pass by and tried to remember the directions her brother had written on a scrap of paper. Rain had blurred the ink, leaving only a few names legible. When the road reached the old bridge, the driver slowed beside a wooden sign almost hidden beneath moss.

An elderly keeper opened the tollhouse door before Mara could knock. He wore a wool coat with brass buttons and held a little notebook in one hand. After studying her map, he pointed to a path that climbed the wooded slope. The lower road had washed out during the storm. Mara thanked him and started uphill, passing a stone wall, a stand of silver birches, and a field where several horses watched her from the far fence.

Near the top, the clouds began to separate. A strip of evening light settled across the valley, brightening the river below. The town stood beyond it, gathered around a square tower with a clock stopped at seven. Mara checked her own watch; it was nearly half past five. At the crossroads she found another sign, carved into a flat stone, and followed it east toward a farmhouse with a red door.

Her brother came out to meet her before she reached the gate. He looked tired, but his smile was unmistakable. Inside, a kettle was warming on the stove. After supper, he showed her the small workshop behind the house. Brass gears, wooden cases, and tiny screws rested in careful rows on a long bench. Mara noticed the clock from the town square among them. Its hands were loose, but the mechanism was clean.

They searched through his notes together. One page mentioned a cracked spring, while a later entry described a missing pin. Neither explained why the clock had stopped after the repair. As her brother opened the back panel, they found a folded scrap of paper tucked between the gears. It carried a date and a name neither of them recognized. They decided to ask the tollhouse keeper about it on their next trip through the valley.
